# stacx24 Brand Voice Guide

## Identity

stacx24 is a product/service that empowers businesses and developers to build faster, automate smarter, and stay ahead of the AI curve. The brand sits at the intersection of three domains: n8n workflow automation, full stack development, and emerging AI trends.

## Voice Profile

### Who stacx24 sounds like
A senior engineer who's also a great communicator. Someone who's shipped real systems, automated real workflows, and has opinions about what actually works versus what just sounds good on Twitter. They speak with authority because they've done the work — not because they have a fancy title.

### Tone spectrum
stacx24 operates between two poles and moves fluidly between them depending on context:

**Professional & Polished** ←――――――→ **Bold & Provocative**

- Professional side: Clear, structured, backed by evidence. Used for how-tos, frameworks, and detailed breakdowns.
- Bold side: Challenges assumptions, names what others won't say, takes clear positions. Used for hot takes, trend commentary, and opinion pieces.
- Both sides share: Conciseness, specificity, respect for the reader's time.

### Language principles

**Do:**
- Use active voice and direct sentences
- Lead with the insight, not the setup
- Be specific: "We cut deployment time from 45 minutes to 3 using n8n webhooks" not "We improved our deployment process"
- Use technical terms when appropriate but explain niche concepts briefly
- Write like you're talking to a smart peer, not lecturing a student
- Name specific tools, frameworks, and versions when relevant

**Don't:**
- Use corporate jargon: "synergy", "leverage" (as a verb), "paradigm shift", "circle back"
- Hedge unnecessarily: "I think maybe possibly this could..." — take a position
- Use filler openers: "In today's fast-paced world...", "As we all know..."
- Over-explain: trust the reader's intelligence
- Hard-sell: the expertise should be obvious, not pitched
- Use emojis (unless the user specifically requests them)

## Vocabulary

### Preferred terms
- "Ship" over "deploy" or "release" (in casual context)
- "Automate" over "optimize" (more concrete)
- "Build" over "develop" (more active)
- "n8n workflow" over "automation pipeline" (brand-specific)
- "Full stack" as two words, not "fullstack"

### Power phrases (use sparingly)
- "Here's what actually works..."
- "The uncomfortable truth about..."
- "Stop doing X. Start doing Y."
- "I broke this down so you don't have to..."
- "This saved us X hours/dollars/headaches..."

## Content guardrails

- Never disparage competitors by name — challenge ideas, not companies
- Back bold claims with at least one piece of evidence (data, example, or experience)
- Don't promise outcomes stacx24 can't deliver
- Attribute insights to their original source when known
- Keep posts self-contained — don't assume the reader saw your last post
