# Story → Lesson Template

## Structure

```
[HOOK — a moment of tension, failure, or surprise that pulls the reader in]

[The situation — brief setup, just enough context]

[The tension — what went wrong, what was at stake]

[The turning point — what changed, what you did differently]

[The resolution — what happened as a result]

[The lesson — the universal takeaway the reader can apply]

[Reflection question or CTA]

```

## Example

```
Our production server went down at 2 AM on a Friday. The fix took 3 minutes. Building the system that made that possible took 6 months.

A client's e-commerce platform was handling 200+ orders per hour. Their monitoring? A Slack channel someone checked "when they remembered."

The outage lasted 47 minutes before anyone noticed. Cost: roughly $8,400 in lost sales.

After that, we built an n8n workflow that monitors uptime, checks API response times every 60 seconds, and auto-triggers a recovery sequence before anyone's phone even buzzes.

The 2 AM incident? The workflow caught the anomaly, restarted the service, and sent a "resolved" notification. I saw it when I woke up.

The lesson: Automation isn't about removing humans from the loop. It's about giving humans their sleep back while the system handles the predictable stuff.

What's the "2 AM problem" in your stack that's still waiting for a human?

#WorkflowAutomation #n8nAutomation #DevOps #stacx24
```

## Tips
- Open with a specific moment, not a generic setup
- Keep the situation/tension section tight — readers want the payoff
- The lesson should be broader than the specific story
- Best when the story involves a real failure or mistake (vulnerability builds trust)
